Chapter 17

Facet separability

Fanning out to a focused judge per construct asserts, on faith, that those constructs are separable. This chapter is the certification under that assertion: whether a decomposition is a real topology, one axis wearing two names, or a prerequisite dressed as a peer.

What it is

Correlation is symmetric. It can say two facets move together; it cannot say that breaking A degrades B while breaking B leaves A intact. That asymmetry is the whole question, and it is invisible to any correlation-shaped measure, the orthogonality audit included.

So the audit intervenes. Over a joint-state panel, events labelled by which facets' defects were injected, it reads each direction separately: how much facet F's score moves when F's own defect is injected, against how much it moves when G's is.

VerdictSignatureWhat it meansWhat to do
separableBoth ratios lowIndependent axes.Keep both.
collinear-collapseBoth high, symmetricOne axis wearing two names.Rejoin. This is the only verdict where rejoining is right.
asymmetric-subsumptionOne high, one lowThe high-ratio facet subsumes the low one: a prerequisite or constituent relationship, not a duplicate.Keep both. The subsumed one is the sharp sub-dimension; the other is the catch-all.
unverifiedUndersampled, a dead own-axis, or a compensatory couplingNot established.Nothing yet. Reported rather than rounded to separable.
The trap this closes A saturation or collinearity signal on its own says "these look redundant, rejoin them." That is right for collinear-collapse and wrong for asymmetric-subsumption, where rejoining destroys a real, independently manipulable axis. Collinear behavior is a hypothesis. Run the factorial before you rejoin.

Why it matters

Two things depend on the answer being right.

A fan-out is a claim about topology. Minting three focused judges per subject asserts those three constructs can be judged apart. If two of them are one axis, you have bought two judges' latency and cost for one axis of signal, and their agreement will read as corroboration when it is tautology.

Reflection depends on it further downstream. Full orthogonality is one of the grant's hard preconditions, and a decomposition that has not certified cannot satisfy it. See What has to hold before a cell reflects.

How to use it

Three reads, in order of cost. The screen proposes a hypothesis, the audit certifies it, the monitor re-checks it cheaply.

Screen for candidates

POST /lens/redundancy/screen
{ "surfaceId": "classify-intent" }

Over per-event score vectors it returns the pairwise collinearity matrix and, for each collinear pair, two things the matrix alone does not give: a scrap-pick naming which twin is the dependent copy, and a residual localization naming the substrate column that holds the copy's only independent variance. Omit dimensionIds and every scored dimension on the surface is screened.

Data-gated. A pair below the minimum sample floor reads undersampled and a dimension with no variance reads no-variance, with a pointer at the saturation audit, since a dimension that never moves is usually a blind judge rather than a redundant one. The screen withholds rather than fires on a thin panel.

Certify the pair

POST /lens/facet-topology/audit
{
  "surfaceId": "classify-intent",
  "facets": [
    { "dimensionId": "hrv-provenance",  "defectColumn": "defect.hrv",   "defectValue": "injected" },
    { "dimensionId": "place-identity",  "defectColumn": "defect.place", "defectValue": "injected" }
  ]
}

Each facets entry is a panel-based expectation: the facet's score dimension, the diagnostic substrate column marking its injected defect, and the value that marks the defect as present. At least two are required, because the question is about a pair.

provenance defaults to all, so a replay or emitted panel is in scope, and correlationIdPrefix scopes to one. options takes the cell-size floor and the ratio cutoffs where a surface needs them; the defaults are conservative.

Read-only, so propose-only. The audit returns a verdict per unordered pair and the operator promotes the split, keeps it, or rejoins.

The audit inherits its panel's validity A facet whose own injected defect does not move its own score reads as a fake subsumption. Those pairs are reported as such rather than scored. The audit is only as good as the ablation underneath it: if the defect you injected was not a defect, nothing downstream of it is a measurement.

Watch the band

POST /lens/pairwise-monitor/run
{ "surfaceId": "classify-intent" }

The audit is expensive and offline. The monitor is the cheap online guard on it. Over per-event defect co-firing it returns, per dimension pair, the co-trigger counts and a phi coupling adjusted for how often each dimension fires on its own, which is what separates real coupling from a shared score floor, plus the signal for a pair collapsing toward collinear while live.

Give it the characterized band as expectedBands and it returns a drift verdict proposing a re-run of the audit. Run the audit with persist: true and it closes the loop itself: the audit also runs the monitor over the same window and appends a durable characterization record merging the verdict with the co-trigger band per pair, which is what the monitor reads back. bandTolerance sets the band's half-width, 0.1 by default.

The monitor proposes drift. It never adjusts a band, because a band that moves itself measures nothing.

When the judge and the producer disagree

POST /lens/contested-cohort/screen
{ "surfaceId": "classify-intent", "dimensionId": "correctness" }

The adjacent question, converging on the same fields. For one judged dimension it partitions events by each candidate substrate-column cohort and tests whether the judge's fail-rate on a cohort value is elevated against the rest of the population, with the family under false-discovery control.

A contested cohort is one where the producer's outputs are systematically rejected. It is reported with the producer's own within-cohort consistency alongside, because a producer that is consistently doing one thing and consistently marked down for it has a different shape from one that is erratic.

The lens cannot adjudicate which party is right, and does not try. It points at the interaction, notes that a focused judge on that field would isolate it, and routes to review. Cohorts below the sample floor are withheld.

Reading the output

Every read here is read-only and propose-only, and three of the attention spine's sources file from them: redundancy-candidate, which mints a rejoin-dimension proposal, plus pairwise-drift and contested-cohort, which mint nothing. A source with no default proposal is one where the remedy is a judgment the lens leaves to the operator.

All four are data-gated and report honest nulls. A withheld pair, an undersampled cohort and an unverified verdict are all distinguishable from a clean result, which is the property that makes a quiet report worth anything.

Where this sits

Upstream is Rubric coverage and de-saturation: a de-saturation fan-out creates the facets this chapter certifies, so the two run as one loop rather than two tools. Alongside it at axis altitude is the orthogonality audit in Variance isolation, which asks whether a declared substrate column explains anything, a different object measured a different way.

The audit procedure, the coupling measure and the tuning constants ship inside the binary. The verdict vocabulary and the record shapes are public contract.
