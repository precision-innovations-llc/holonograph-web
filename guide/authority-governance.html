Chapter 23

Authority governance

Authority is a reading the lens derives and never writes. Two dispositions over it belong to the operator: releasing the hold a tear puts on a surface’s soak window, and setting what one release covers. Both are gated, both are attributed to a named operator, and both leave a record the compactor will not touch.

What it is

Three parts: the gate every privileged write passes through, the acknowledgement that releases a tear, and the standing policy that says what an acknowledgement covers.

The approver gate

Every privileged write on the lens carries an approverId and is refused without one. A self-asserted id that disagrees with the verified operator on the request is refused too, so the stamped identity is not the caller’s claim about itself.

Who may act is a separate question, and the lens does not answer it. Tier policy lives in the operator’s identity system, so authorization is a pluggable resolver, absent by default. With none wired, any identified operator may proceed and the action is stamped with their id. Wire one and the same endpoints enforce its decision, denying with 403. The lens records who acts before it constrains who may act.

The resolver is asked about one action at a time, and the action vocabulary is deliberately fine-grained, so an identity system can grant the cheap dispositions widely and the expensive ones narrowly.

Two of the nine are authority’s, and they are separate on purpose. More people can reasonably be trusted to release one tear than to change how every future release is recorded.

What a tear holds

A tear is a served or judge model value appearing on a gated surface for the first time. The surface’s authority soak window anchors at the previous stack change, held back to the segment before the latest unacknowledged tear, and a tear never self-clears.

So a tear is not a notification. Releasing it moves the window forward, which can let a torn surface re-qualify and let its pre-tear span be summarised. That is a disposition over authority, which is why it has a gate of its own and an audit record rather than a dismiss button.

Why it matters

The authority reading is only worth what its evidence window is worth. A window that slid forward over a model change on its own would be reporting one instrument’s record as another’s, and nothing downstream could tell. Holding the window and making an operator say, on the record, that the span is still one subject is the property that keeps the reading meaningful.

The mode that governs those releases is a standing disposition, not a per-call choice. Under the default, one acknowledgement covers every earlier open tear on the surface. Under per-tear, each tear takes its own, oldest first. An identity system can only express the distinction between releasing a tear and changing that mode if the two are separate actions.

How to use it

Read the open tears

GET /lens/authority/tears?surfaceId=classify-intent

Every tear on the authority-read surfaces, newest first, each with the instant and event id of the tear, the stack members whose value changed, the stack tuple before and at the tear, and whether an acknowledgement covers it. Beside them the acknowledgement log, also newest first, and the granularity a new acknowledgement would take now. surfaceId narrows the read.

Release one

POST /lens/authority/tear/acknowledge
{
  "surfaceId": "classify-intent",
  "approverId": "ops.rivera",
  "tearEventId": "evt_01H...",
  "note": "same family, re-validated against the fixture set"
}

201 with the minted event id and the record. Omit tearEventId and the lens picks for you: the surface’s latest unacknowledged tear under watermark, its oldest under per-tear.

A surface with no gated decision in the current contract is not an authority-read surface, and acknowledging on it is a 404 rather than a silent no-op. So is naming an event that is not a tear.

Retries and duplicates The acknowledgement’s event id is derived from the surface and the tear, so resubmitting the same acknowledgement is a keep-first no-op in the store. Acknowledging a tear that is already covered is a 409, and under per-tear, acknowledging out of order is a 409 that names the older open tear.

Set what a release covers

GranularityOne acknowledgement covers
watermarkThe acknowledged tear and every earlier tear on the surface, in one log record. The default.
per-tearOnly the acknowledged tear, one log record each, applied oldest first.
 
PUT /lens/authority/policy
{
  "policy": { "tearAckGranularity": "per-tear" },
  "approverId": "ops.rivera",
  "basePolicyId": "ap-9c21f0b47de3a815",
  "note": "audit asked for one record per tear"
}

basePolicyId is the record the operator reviewed, and null when the policy in force came from the file seed or the defaults. The schema is strict: an unknown key is refused rather than ignored, so a typo cannot read as a policy that does not apply.

Compare-and-swap on the write If the policy moved since the operator reviewed it, the write is refused with authority_policy_conflict rather than overwriting someone else’s change. Reload, re-review, re-submit. The retention policy works the same way.

GET /lens/authority/policy returns the policy in force, its source, the operator record behind it or null, whether this backend can write one at all, and whether a configured file seed is being ignored because an operator policy exists. Resolution order is the operator’s persisted policy, then the file seed, then the defaults.

A backend that keeps no durable authority-policy store answers the PUT with 501 and reports that in writable. The filesystem backend keeps one. There the policy in force is the file seed at APERTURE_AUTHORITY_POLICY_PATH, or the defaults.

Where the operator does this

The authority-policy desk in Curate holds the editor, the diff against the policy in force, and the history. The tears card in the Lens Health panel holds the open tears and the acknowledgement. Both go through the same gate as the API, and the desk is read-only on a backend that cannot persist a policy. See The dashboard.

Reading the output

acknowledged on a tear is derived rather than stored: true when a watermark acknowledgement at or after it exists on the surface, or a per-tear acknowledgement of exactly it. The log sits beside the tears rather than inside them, so who released what, when, under which mode, and with what note is readable on its own.

Each record keeps the granularity in force when it was made. Changing the mode changes what the next acknowledgement covers and never reinterprets a past one.

Acknowledgements are appended as analysis.authority-tear-acknowledged events: server-stamped, append-only, and exempt from compaction. The history behind the policy is append-only too, each record naming the policy it replaced, the operator who approved it, and when.

Where this sits

Authority is the reading: the six states, the stack, and what withdraws a grant. This chapter is the disposition over it. The gate and its action vocabulary are shared with the retention desk in Compaction & husking and the multiplex run in The multiplexer, which is why the table above reaches past authority.

The state ontology, the record shapes, the policy schema and the action identifiers are public contract. Which spans a released window then covers, and how the states are derived from it, lives in the lens.
