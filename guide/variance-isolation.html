Chapter 13

Variance isolation and declared axes

When your evaluation scores move, some of the movement is real change in your system and some is just the noise of measuring with a language model. Variance isolation returns a cleaned reading: how much of the spread reflects your system versus measurement noise, plus a verdict that tells you whether to trust the reading yet.

What it is

A per-surface, per-dimension analysis over a window that returns two things: a cleaned variance figure and a confidence verdict. The verdict is the output to read first. It tells you whether the cleaned figure is trustworthy right now, or whether the reading needs more data (or different data) before you act on it.

What you get

A cleaned variance figure and a confidence verdict. There are four:

VerdictMeaning
viableTrustworthy. Act on it.
degenerateToo few distinct conditions to separate signal from noise. Gather more data.
contaminatedInputs weren't held constant enough. Re-run under controlled conditions.
instrument-limitedMeasurement noise dominates. Don't trust the reading yet.

The verdict is what decides whether the number is worth acting on. A viable reading is the one you use; the other three are diagnostic. Each names why the reading isn't ready and what would fix it.

How to run it

Three endpoints, in order. The first produces the reading; the second gives it a verdict; the third governs what happens to it.

1. Produce the reading

POST /lens/decomposeVariance
Authorization: Bearer <token>
x-holonograph-run-mode: production
Content-Type: application/json

{
  "surfaceId": "classify-intent",
  "dimensionId": "correctness",
  "since": "2026-06-01T00:00:00Z",
  "until": "2026-07-05T00:00:00Z"
}

Response: an analysis record you'll reference by eventId in the next step.

{
  "eventId": "evt_01H...",
  "surfaceId": "classify-intent",
  "dimensionId": "correctness"
}

2. Get the verdict

POST /lens/decomposition/evaluate
Content-Type: application/json

{ "targetEventId": "evt_01H..." }

Response: the confidence verdict and guidance for what to do next.

3. Govern the result

Once you've seen the verdict, decide what to do with the reading. Governance is one of four actions:

POST /lens/decomposition/lifecycle
Content-Type: application/json

{
  "targetEventId": "evt_01H...",
  "status": "lock",
  "approverId": "brian",
  "pinnedLabel": "billing-triage-baseline-2026-Q3"
}
StatusEffect
quarantineHold the reading aside; don't use it in downstream comparisons until reviewed.
approveAccept the reading as trustworthy. Requires an approverId.
lockFreeze this reading as the reference against a pinnedLabel. Requires an approverId and the pinnedLabel.
rejectDiscard the reading. Requires an approverId and a comment.

The lifecycle response returns the persisted status alongside the original targetEventId. Governance decisions are themselves substrate events. They become part of the record, attributable in future drift readings like any other change.

What to do with each verdict

VerdictNext action
viable Read the cleaned variance figure. If you want to hold this specific reading as a reference, lock it with a pinnedLabel. Otherwise approve and move on.
degenerate The window doesn't have enough distinct conditions for the analysis to separate signal from noise. Widen the window, or drive more variety through the surface before re-running.
contaminated The inputs weren't held constant enough to trust the reading. Re-run under controlled conditions. A replay through the surface at a fixed input panel is the usual fix.
instrument-limited Measurement noise dominates whatever real movement is present. Don't trust the reading yet; if the reading matters, quarantine it while you widen the window or reduce input variability.

Declared axes

The decomposition says how much of the movement is real. The next question is which of your declared substrate columns explains any of it. An axis is a column you declared because you believed it accounted for outcome variance; an audit says whether the record agrees.

Audit what you declared

POST /lens/orthogonality/audit
{ "surfaceId": "classify-intent", "dimensionId": "correctness" }

Grades every declared axis on how much of the dimension it accounts for, returning confirmed, inert, insufficient-data or excluded. A pure read: no calibration, no gate, no model call.

The confirmed decision is false-discovery controlled across the entire axis family. Each axis carries both its original and adjusted pValue, and the fdr summary reports the trade-off.

The audit is lifecycle-aware, which is the axis-altitude twin of surface retirement. A forward-declared or deprecated axis is excluded, since a deliberate stub is not a failure. An inert active axis carries a recommendedStatus of forward-declared, which defers it and excludes it from evaluation, and is reversible. The lens recommends; the operator decides.

When the demotion hint is withheld The demotion hint fires only where an axis has no observed consumer. Two count. Every active axis is tested against all dimensions in the window, so one that explains a different dimension is protected. So is one a live conformance fixture references in a guard, an assertion or its required markers: a state-conditional guard that explains no scored dimension is still doing work for a deterministic check.

Find the undeclared ones

POST /lens/orthogonality/propose
{ "surfaceId": "classify-intent", "dimensionId": "correctness" }

The mirror of the audit. Where the audit grades what you declared, this looks for what you are already emitting and have not declared, and proposes it as a candidate axis.

It needs a noise floor for the pair first, so calibrate before calling it. Then it decomposes the window, aggregates substrate emission over those same events, and takes the emitted-but-undeclared columns as the candidate set. Three gates have to pass: enough scored data, agent variance established above zero, and at least one undeclared column. Zero candidates is a 200 with a diagnostics block naming the gate that held, not an error.

Each candidate is narrated and enqueued on the approver gate. The narration is prose only: the evidence and the target ids are copied verbatim from the deterministic candidate, and the narrator has no channel through which to emit a number. The lens never declares the axis. Declaring the column and re-decomposing is the operator's.

Tell an axis from a confound

POST /lens/orthogonality/audit/compare
{
  "surfaceId": "classify-intent",
  "dimensionId": "correctness",
  "correlationIdPrefix": "replay:nov-panel:"
}

The audit run twice: once over all production, once over the input-constant panel the prefix selects, then diffed per axis. An axis confirmed on production that collapses under input constancy was never explaining the outcome; its effect size was entanglement between the lens and the input mix. One confirmed in both is robust.

The response names the confounded columns and the robust ones, with a per-axis diff carrying each read's verdict, its effect size, and the delta between them. The panel has to exist in the substrate already, as a replay whose scores you re-posted under that prefix. An empty panel reads as insufficient per axis rather than failing.

Orthogonality is symmetric: it can say an axis explains variance, never that breaking A degrades B while B leaves A intact. That asymmetry is a different instrument at a lower altitude, in Facet separability.

Where this sits

Two of the attention spine's sources file from the axis reads: orthogonality-hypothesis, which proposes declaring a column, and primary-axis-orthogonality, reported as a confounded axis. Both are partitioned per source, because pooling two agents' score populations manufactures correlation neither of them shows. See The attention spine.
