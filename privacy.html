3. Information collected through the Site

Information you submit through the contact form. The form collects the fields presented at the time of submission, which may include name, email address, organization, and the content of your message. Submission is voluntary. Do not submit confidential, sensitive, or regulated information through the form. See Section 6 of the Terms and Conditions regarding unsolicited submissions.

Server and log data. Hosting and content delivery infrastructure automatically records technical data such as IP address, browser type and version, referring page, requested URL, and timestamp. This data is generated by the act of requesting a page and is used for security, abuse prevention, and diagnostics.

Analytics. The Site uses Google Analytics (GA4), configured in cookieless mode, and Cloudflare Web Analytics, which is cookieless by design, to measure aggregate traffic. Neither sets cookies, persists an identifier across visits, or is used for advertising, retargeting, or cross-site tracking.

Cookies. The Site’s own pages set no cookies. Google Analytics runs with client storage disabled, so no analytics cookies are written, and no advertising, retargeting, or cross-site tracking cookies are used.

One cookie is set by the Nigel demonstration described in Section 5, and only once you send Nigel a message. It is named nigel_sid and holds a random session identifier, used to keep your conversation together and to enforce the limits published in the Terms and Conditions. It cannot be read by scripts on the page, is sent only over encrypted connections, and expires 24 hours after it is set. Because it is issued by the demonstration’s own service rather than by holonograph.ai, some browsers block it by default, in which case your conversation is not carried from one message to the next.

The Site also stores a small number of display preferences in your browser, such as whether the Nigel panel is open and whether the embedded lens view refreshes itself. These stay on your device and are not sent to Precision Innovations.

4. Information that is not collected

Precision Innovations considers the following worth stating plainly, because the architecture of the product makes it true rather than merely promised.

  • The Holonograph software runs as a self-hosted binary inside the licensee's own environment. It is not a hosted service.
  • Evaluation data, prompts, model responses, traces, lens configurations, and results processed by an installation remain inside the perimeter of whoever operates that installation. They are not transmitted to Precision Innovations.
  • The Site does not receive telemetry from installed copies of the software.
  • The Site does not collect payment card information. Any commercial transaction occurs outside the Site.
  • Precision Innovations does not sell personal information and does not share personal information for cross-context behavioral advertising.

The demonstration described in Section 5 is not an exception to the points above. It is an instance of them. Precision Innovations operates its own installation of Holonograph, and the data that installation holds stays inside the perimeter of Precision Innovations. What differs is where that perimeter has been drawn. Precision Innovations has chosen to draw it around a public page, which means that a visitor interacting with Nigel is a data source inside the perimeter of Precision Innovations rather than the operator of a perimeter of their own. Read every assurance about data remaining inside the perimeter as an assurance to the operator of the installation. When you type into Nigel, the operator is Precision Innovations, not you.

5. The public lens view and Nigel

The Site publishes an interactive view of a lens, including an agent named Nigel that visitors may interact with.

Input is stored. What you type to Nigel, what Nigel returns, and technical metadata about the exchange are captured and stored in the lens. The lens is the record. Storage is not incidental to the demonstration, it is the demonstration.

Stored interactions may be public. Stored exchanges may be displayed in the public lens view and may therefore be readable by other visitors. Treat anything you type to Nigel as published.

Third-party processing. In order to produce a response, your input is transmitted to a third-party large-language-model provider and processed under their terms. It also passes through the demonstration’s cloud hosting provider. Both are identified in Section 7.

What not to submit. Do not type confidential information, trade secrets, credentials, health or financial information, personal information about yourself or anyone else, or any regulated data into Nigel. There is no need to identify yourself in order to use the demonstration, and doing so is discouraged.

No identity is requested. Nigel requires no account, no login, and no identifying information of any kind. Precision Innovations does not ask who you are and has no interest in finding out.

This is not the same as anonymity, and the difference is stated here rather than glossed. Rate limiting requires holding a one-way hash of your source address and a session identifier, and a session identifier links your exchanges to one another. Free-text input is also self-identifying whenever a visitor chooses to make it so. Stored interactions are therefore pseudonymous rather than anonymous: they are not linked to a name by Precision Innovations, but they are capable of being linked to a person by someone holding the right external information, and they are capable of containing whatever a visitor typed.

The session record that enforces those limits holds your conversation together with the hashed source address. Each exchange stored in the lens carries a short identifier derived from the session, which groups a visitor’s exchanges together in the public lens view and allows Precision Innovations to match them to the session record. The hashed source address is never stored in the lens and is never shown in the public lens view.

Rate limiting data. To enforce the limits published in the Terms and Conditions and to prevent abuse, the Site records a salted one-way hash of the source IP address (the address itself is not stored for this purpose), a session identifier, and request timestamps and counts. This data is used for rate limiting, abuse prevention, and diagnostics, and is not displayed in the public lens view.

Retention. Stored interactions are retained for 180 days. Rate limiting records are retained for 30 days. Precision Innovations may remove, redact, or delete any stored interaction at any time.

Removal requests. If you submitted something you would like removed from the public lens view, write to privacy@holonograph.ai with enough detail to locate the exchange. Requests are honored where the exchange can be identified. Because interactions are stored without accounts and without identity verification, Precision Innovations may be unable to confirm that a given exchange is yours, and may decline a request it cannot verify.

Legal basis. Where the GDPR or UK GDPR applies, the legal basis for processing demonstration input is legitimate interests in operating and demonstrating the product and in securing the Site against abuse. You are asked not to submit personal information, and the demonstration is designed so that none is needed.
