Agentic governance

What correct-looking behavior can conceal

Agentic systems create an endless stream of interpretive data, often then acting upon their own inferences. This is what makes them both incredibly useful and difficult to validate. A system can be right for the wrong reason, confidently claim no access to data it can reach, answer questions without using the tool intended to provide the answer, and more. Often these pass the casual user as operating normally and would not be reported. These issues can compound, eventually combining to create larger, noticeable system glitches of which the source is notoriously difficult to trace. Holonograph monitors for 25 such known issues and alerts both the dashboard and builder agent of issues worthy of attention.

The risks with interpretive data

Where interpretation becomes authority

Much of software’s value comes from interpreting data and acting on the results. Regexes, fuzzy matching, forms, and language models all contribute interpretations that can influence downstream decisions.

As AI builders accelerate software development, those interpretations can acquire consequential influence at machine speed without first establishing whether the assumptions behind them are justified.

Authority governance

Authority, derived from the record

A decision can be permitted, approved, confidently made, and even correct without having established authority.

Holonograph separates those concepts. It evaluates whether a decision surface’s declared prerequisites are satisfied by its recorded evidence, and re-derives that authority whenever the record is read. No actor confers it. It exists only while its conditions hold.

Definition

Authority is a state held per decision surface: the declared prerequisites for a specified consequential transition are satisfied on the record as it stands.

Guide, chapter 22 →

The authority lifecycle

Authority is established, observed, and revocable

One decision surface, followed through its record: qualification, a grant, a dependency change, and qualification again. Select any state, or change a dependency once the grant holds.

A granted state establishes that this contract is satisfied on this record. It does not certify the decision correct in general.

Five neighboring words

What authority is, and is not

Permission

An actor is allowed to act.

Approval

An actor affirmatively accepts something.

Correctness

A proposition matches an applicable reference.

Confidence

A degree of belief, or a measurement’s precision.

Authority

The declared prerequisites for this transition are satisfied on the record.

Humans, builders and automated sources all produce events on the record. An operator approving a decision is approval, recorded as such. It does not move the state.

Seams

Where inference becomes a decision

An interpreted value driving a decision that governs a consequential transition is a seam. It is permanent. You can close it and you can monitor it. You cannot validate it away.

What is a seam?

An edge from interpretation to decision

A seam is the path a value travels from an interpretive source to the decision point that consumes it.

Interpretives

A model output, a classifier, an approximate match, a pattern over free text, an external feed.

Decision points

A branch, threshold, pattern, or key that acts on the value.

Open seams

A decision point with no answer for what it does not recognize. The defective state.

Abstention

The defined path for what a decision does not recognize. A decision point with one is attended.

Consequence

The transition a decision governs. It is what makes a seam matter.

Provenance

Where a request came from is as important as the request itself.

Real-time attendanceBuilder hooks report each seam into the session that wrote it, at the moment of writing.

A model is a seam whose interpreter moves

A pattern does not rewrite itself between runs. A model can be re-routed behind an unchanged name, and the instrument scoring it is usually another model. The lifecycle is the same, with monitoring properties unique to LLM seams.

Observability is not Attribution

What moved with a changed score?

A value changing is not yet an answer. Holonograph decomposes a score by attributing each part to your agent, the model, or custom evaluation system, and separates real change from noise, quantifying how much each source moved with that number. This decomposition is the method under patent application. It turns the changed score into an actionable report. Substrate labeling further identifies which component of your agent’s substrate contributed to the agent’s part: lessons, skills, code, and prompt are each separated and attributed.

An observed shift

The instrument

your agent

The prompts, tools, and logic you built.

the model

The provider’s weights, version, and token relationships. Aspects you can’t control.

your eval

The judge and/or rubric doing the scoring. If a judge, also subject to the same model black box as above.

noise

Sampling and chance, identified and isolated.

Past a certain scale, a metric that moves is not an answer. Attribution is.

One behavior tracked across every version

The longitudinal substrate

Attribution requires memory

A number can move without the behavior moving. When the model is updated, or the lens that scores it is revised, the metric drifts while the underlying behavior does not change. Holonograph versions every lens and keeps the full history, so each reading is checked against the exact lens that produced it. The outcome is a clear behavioral attribution to the model, the substrate, or the judges themselves.

The model you asked for and the model that answered are two different facts. A vendor can re-route behind an unchanged alias. Holonograph records both sides, so that change has a name instead of showing up as unexplained drift. The judge has the same two sides.

The attribution matrix

A release moved several scores. The matrix reads each declared dimension against the axis of each source, in the terms the dashboard uses: the gap in points, whether chance explains it, and what stands in the way of attribution. A gap is a candidate. Attribution holds only where the record separates the sources.

Rows

Dimensions as declared in the lens, such as title_quality or tool_discipline.

Columns

Each source is read on its own axis: the substrate, light_source_served_model, judge identity (rubric · model · scope), and the noise floor.

Cells

The gap in points between groups on that axis. A gap is a candidate for attribution, not a causal share. Brighter cells hold larger gaps.

Not chance

A gap this wide would show up by chance less than once in 1,000 samples. Here, tool_discipline moved with the substrate.

Orthogonality

The axis was declared one that shouldn’t move the score at all, and the score moved with it.

Confounded axis

The light source and the judge changed in the same window, so the title_quality gap cannot be split between them. Separating the two takes its own probe.

Concentrated, smooth

From the judge transfer curve. Concentrated: most scores land on a single value, and the judge is barely discriminating. Smooth: scores spread across many values, a graded response.

Within noise

A gap smaller than the noise floor for that dimension.

Noise floor

Per-call variation, measured as a calibrated floor for each dimension.

Lens facets

When a rubric stops discriminating

When one judge scores many concerns at once, its scale can come to rest at an anchor. A perfect ten and a nine stop being different readings, and a failure no longer tells you how many things went wrong, only that something did. Holonograph reports that condition rather than passing the number through as an ordinary score. Where a rubric resolves into separate facets of rubric, judge, and context scope, each is grouped and attributed on its own, and where two move together the apparatus says so instead of guessing between them.

What Does an LLM Judge Actually Measure?

The Role of Evaluation Frames in Construct Discrimination. Brian Kerrigan, 2026.

The finding

An LLM judge can produce a coherent rationale and a stable-looking score without establishing that the score measures the defect the operator intended. Two prospectively governed studies tested controlled defects across three fixed evaluator configurations: 4,800 judgments under ordinary holistic scoring, then 8,640 across three evaluation frames.

Under ordinary holistic scoring, no configuration established the preregistered target-discrimination criterion. Under target-bound scoring, all three did. Before relying on a judge for consequential inference, its ability to discriminate the relevant construct should be shown under the intended evaluation frame.

Calls passed through dedicated local Holonograph Lens instances, which supplied call-time correlation, route and served-model identity, substrate stamps, cohort tags, and immutable digest-only events. Raw requests and responses stayed outside the Lens.

Disclosure. Holonograph is developed by the study's author. The manuscript states this in its own methods, and so does this page.
